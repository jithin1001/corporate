❓ Classroom Exploration: What happens to EPS if a company uses debt instead of equity?

Debt financing involves contractual interest payments which are tax-deductible. While debt introduces financial risk, it avoids issuing new shares. When EBIT is high, debt magnifies EPS; when EBIT is low, fixed interest can sharply depress EPS!

Find the operating profit level at which two financing structures generate identical earnings per share.

📐 Mathematical Formula for Indifference Point Standard Corporate Finance Formula

At the indifference EBIT, the EPS of Plan 1 equals the EPS of Plan 2:

[ (EBIT − I₁) × (1 − T) − PD₁ ]
N₁
=
[ (EBIT − I₂) × (1 − T) − PD₂ ]
N₂

Where: EBIT = Indifference Operating Profit, I₁, I₂ = Fixed Interest charges, T = Tax Rate, PD₁, PD₂ = Preference Dividends, N₁, N₂ = Total Ordinary Shares in issue.

⚠️ Academic Disclaimer: These are general economic characteristics. The actual effect depends on the company's financial position, tax rate, financing covenants, and underlying operational volatility.

1. Why Debt Can Increase EPS (Leverage Magnification)

When operating profit (EBIT) is sufficiently high, using debt is advantageous because creditors receive only a fixed interest return. The remaining surplus profit belongs entirely to ordinary shareholders. Because no new shares are issued, that expanding surplus is divided among the existing share base, causing EPS to surge!

2. Why Debt Increases Financial Risk

Interest is a legal, contractual obligation. Regardless of whether sales drop or economic recessions hit, debt interest and principal installments must be paid on time. If operating profits fall below interest obligations (EBIT < Interest), the company suffers losses and risks loan default or bankruptcy.

3. Why Equity Can Reduce Financial Risk

Ordinary equity does not require compulsory periodic interest payments or fixed principal redemption dates. If earnings decline, the Board of Directors can reduce or omit dividend payouts without legal default. However, issuing new equity shares increases the total share count, which can dilute EPS.

4. The Corporate Tax Shield Explained

Under corporate tax law, interest expense is an allowable deduction against revenue before calculating taxable profit. In contrast, equity dividends are distributed out of post-tax profit. Therefore, every ₹1 of interest saves ₹1 × Tax Rate in corporate income taxes, reducing the net effective cost of debt!
